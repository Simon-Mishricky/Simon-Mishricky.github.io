Job Market Paper

Asset Price Dispersion, Monetary Policy and Macroprudential Regulation

Sole authored · Draft available

Abstract

Following the 2007–2009 Global Financial Crisis, sustained monetary expansion and tighter financial regulation have left financial markets thinner, less resilient and more prone to instability. This paper develops a monetary model of decentralised financial exchange to account for these outcomes. The framework links search frictions and costly posting to the joint effects of monetary and regulatory policy on asset prices, quoting behaviour and market stability. Increases in inflation or posting costs reduce quoting intensity, widen the distribution of executable prices and raise the probability of trading breakdowns. The model replicates key post-crisis patterns such as wider spreads, higher execution costs and an increased likelihood of flash-crash events, showing that the interaction between monetary and regulatory policy can unintentionally increase financial market fragility.

Asset Pricing with Collateral and Resale Premia

Sole authored · Draft available on request

Abstract

Asset prices respond ambiguously to monetary policy. Easing sometimes lowers dividend yields, as in traditional and collateral or liquidity-based monetary asset pricing models, and sometimes raises them, as in monetary resale premium models. This paper reconciles these views by developing a monetary over-the-counter model where assets serve both as collateral for goods and asset purchases and as resaleable securities. The framework generates both collateral and resale premia, explaining why dividend yields can move in opposite directions under monetary easing or tightening. I also show that policy remains effective in the cashless limit, and that the cashless limit equilibrium differs from the nonmonetary equilibrium, which highlights that the role of money cannot be abstracted from asset pricing.

Optimal Competitive Dispersion

With Sephorah Mangin and Yizhuo Zhang · Draft available on request

Abstract

Consumers often pay different prices for the same good. One reason is that the price paid by a consumer depends on the number of sellers competing for that particular consumer, so prices reflect local competition. In general the average price depends on both the degree of competition, meaning the average number of competing sellers, and the degree of competitive dispersion, meaning the dispersion in the number of competing sellers. This paper asks what the optimal degree of competitive dispersion is. In a simple model with search frictions, we find that buyers and sellers do not generally agree. In less competitive markets buyers want more dispersion than sellers. In more competitive markets sellers want more dispersion than buyers. Only in a narrow range of intermediate markets do the two agree.

sparsepea.py

Open source · Python · 2025

A package for solving DSGE models with the parameterised expectations algorithm on sparse grids. It is built to keep high-dimensional dynamic models tractable without the memory cost of tensor-product grids.
